    Design reference

    Prose

    Every word Oxagen writes follows one set of English rules: the interface, the docs, the page specs, the audit prompts, issues, and commit messages. The voice is a senior engineer who has read the logs and is telling you what happened. State the fact, name the number, stop.

    Sources the clear-prose skill, oxagen-branding voice.md and words.md, issue #87 Enforced by tools/check-copy.mjs

    Voice

    Five traits, and the interface never explains its own design.

    TraitWhat it meansExample
    PlainShort words and short sentences. A technical word is fine where it is the right word, on the page that defines it.Every frame is hash-chained to the one before it.
    SpecificNumbers, names, and paths where another product would put an adjective. A number is measured and dated, or it stays out.34 of the 52 tools on the toolbelt were not called once in 1,340 runs.
    UnhurriedNo urgency and no excitement. Nothing is "finally here".Budget saved. It applies from the next check.
    DryUnderstatement over emphasis. If a sentence reads stronger with the intensifier gone, remove it.Blocked once, held on the second stop.
    Honest to the recordSay what the evidence supports and stop. Scope every control claim.Observe mode records the call without enforcing it.
    No design reasoning in the interface. A page states what is true and what you can do. It does not argue for its own design, weigh its own words, or tell you what a figure is not. The copy review (issue #87) removed lines such as It is not a result and says more than that.

    Rules that never bend

    Ten rules apply to every surface.

    1. No em dashes and no en dashes as separators.

      No double hyphen standing in for one either. Use a period, a comma, a colon, or parentheses.

    2. No exclamation points.
    3. Active voice, actor first.

      "The rule denies the push," never "the push is denied by the rule."

    4. One idea per sentence.

      A semicolon means two sentences. Write them as two.

    5. Sentence case

      For headings, buttons, labels, tabs, menu items, and table headers.

    6. The Oxford comma.

      "Access, budget, and equipment."

    7. Say "you", never "users".

      In product copy, name Oxagen or stella. Never "we".

    8. Numbers over adjectives.

      "Two hooks, sixty seconds" beats "fast setup." Numerals for data, words when a number opens a sentence.

    9. Every claim is one the record can back.

      Never strengthen a claim past the evidence. An allowed request means a rule allowed it, and nothing more.

    10. Open on the reader's situation.

      Start with what the reader is looking at or trying to do, not with what the product is.

    Headings and labels

    A heading names the thing, and a caption states one fact.

    • A heading, panel title, tab, table header, or label is a plain noun or noun phrase: "Spend by area", "Repository", "Toolbelt".
    • No label carries a comma, a mid-dot, or a "not" or "never" contrast. Mac banned the aphoristic style on 2026-09-21. It is hard to scan and too dense.
    • Subtext under a heading is one sentence or nothing. A caption states one fact.
    • A button starts with a verb and names its object when the verb alone is unclear: "Set a budget", "Retire agent", "Steer".
    • Descriptive header captions and sort-order labels are cut. The table header's sort arrow says the order.

    Punctuation

    Periods and commas do the work.

    • Separators. A period, a comma, a colon, or parentheses. Never an em dash, an en dash between words, or a double hyphen.
    • Semicolons. None in anything a customer reads. Two ideas are two sentences.
    • Colons introduce a list or a definition.
    • The mid-dot may separate values in a data line, such as a card's meta line. Never in a heading, label, caption, button, or sentence.
    • Ranges. In a sentence, write "10 to 20". A pager figure uses an en dash with no spaces: 1–10 of 23 (page 1 of 3).
    • Quotes. Double quotes for a quoted string. Code, keys, and paths go in monospace instead of quotes.
    • Exclamation points. None.

    Empty values

    A missing value says it is missing, and why when it can.

    • An empty cell or value is — in --dim. A column of counts with a few dashes still reads as numbers.
    • Where a reason fits, say it: "not recorded for harness runs", "none yet", "no agent".
    • A field no source backs today reads "not recorded". It never shows a fixture value or a plausible guess.
    • Never render undefined, null, NaN, or an empty slot such as Task —.
    • Never print a zero the record did not give. Zero means the record says zero.

    Words to avoid

    Cut these on sight.

    Words that mean nothing

    seamless, robust, powerful, revolutionary, cutting-edge, next-generation, game-changing, best-in-class, world-class, enterprise-grade, comprehensive, holistic, end-to-end, turnkey, frictionless, effortless, intelligent, smart, magic

    Intensifiers

    very, really, truly, genuinely, incredibly, extremely, deeply, highly, super

    Emotional and fear sells

    excited, thrilled, proud, delighted, love, passionate, finally, at last, imagine, liability, exposed, unchecked, rogue, dangerous, protect, safeguard

    Category words owned by others

    Mission Control, observability, evals, guardrails, trust layer, safety layer, AI ops, LLMOps, AgentOps, orchestration as a category

    Overclaims

    guaranteed, always and never about outcomes, 100%, zero, eliminates, proven for anything a dod did, verified for anything a model did

    Product words Oxagen does not use

    AI-powered, LLM-powered, agentic as an adjective for the product, copilot, autonomous as a compliment

    Words that hand over the keys

    connect your agent to, give the agent access to, full access, the agent's API key, the agent's token, one-click connect, auto-approve as a feature, least privilege

    Claims

    Say exactly what the record supports.

    • Scope every control claim. "For actions routed through Oxagen" or "on governed calls". Never "on every call" or "on every run" alone.
    • Observe mode is recorded, not enforced. Say which one applies.
    • An answer means only what it says. An allowed request means a rule allowed it, not that the action was safe.
    • Credentials. For mediated connections, Oxagen uses the connection credential on the agent's behalf and the agent does not receive it. Never promise that an agent has nothing to leak.
    • Savings. No savings claim without the measured workload and its conditions.
    • Compliance. No SOC 2 claim beyond what the actual report states.
    • People. An operator review reports what the record shows, with each habit's definition beside it. It never grades or ranks the person.

    Accessible writing

    Words a screen reader speaks follow the same rules as words on screen.

    • A link names its destination. Never "click here" or "learn more" alone.
    • An icon-only button has an aria-label with the action and its object: "Close approvals", "Remove item 2".
    • A row or card that opens something is labelled with what it opens: "Open wo_01K5RS7M4N".
    • A list of chips carries a hidden , between items (SEP), so it reads and copies as a list.
    • Never point by color or position ("the red badge", "on the right"). Name the thing.
    • State is a word. A color or a shape repeats it and never replaces it.

    Docs, specs, and internal writing

    Internal text is read by someone who has to act on it.

    • Docs. Second person, imperative, one step per sentence. The working command first, the reason in one line after. No marketing language.
    • Page specs and audit prompts. Quote the interface copy exactly, so an audit can compare it character by character.
    • Issues. The problem, the file paths, how to reproduce it, the constraints already found, which pillar it moves, and what done looks like.
    • Pull requests and commits. What changed and why, in plain sentences. Name each unrelated fix on its own.
    • Code comments. The same rules. A comment says why the code is the way it is.

    What the 2026-09-25 review of the copy rules found.

    fixedA retired term in three views

    The Work empty state, the Intake dialog, and the connection wizard said "issue provider". They say "issue tracker" now, and the page specs and check-tasks.mjs follow.

    openThe copy check fails on main

## Inputs

1. mockups/prose.html in the roadmap repo. Read it in full: voice, the ten rules, headings and labels, casing and names, punctuation, numbers, empty values, vocabulary, retired and internal terms, words to avoid, claims, interface strings, identifiers and keys, spelling, and accessible writing.
2. tools/check-copy.mjs. Its RULES and PROSE_ONLY arrays are the automated subset. Run the same patterns over the build's rendered text.
3. The build's string sources: components, locale files, error messages, empty states, toasts, dialogs, tooltips, and aria-labels.
4. Routes to sample: every page in the navigation, every tab, every dialog, the approvals drawer, the assistant, each page's empty, error, and denied state, and the phone shell at 390 by 844.

## Procedure

Record PASS, FAIL, or N/A for every check. Cite evidence: the route, the element, the exact text, and the source file and line.

1. Automated patterns. Run every check-copy pattern over each route's visible text, with pre and code hidden, and the raw key pattern with .mono hidden too. Every match is a FAIL. Include views check-copy never opens: empty states and every dialog.
2. Punctuation. No em dash, no en dash between words, no double hyphen, no semicolon, and no exclamation point in visible text or aria-labels. A pager figure such as 1–10 of 23 is allowed.
3. Headings and labels. Every heading, panel title, tab, table header, label, and caption is sentence case and a plain noun, with no comma, no mid-dot, and no "not" or "never" contrast. Subtext under a heading is one sentence or nothing.
4. Buttons. Each starts with a verb, is sentence case, and names its object when the verb alone is unclear. Count gold buttons per screen: more than one is a FAIL.
5. Brand and names. The interface writes oxagen and stella in lowercase. Acronyms keep capitals (PR, CI, US, EU, APAC). Other products are written as their owners write them. "Mission Control" anywhere is a FAIL.
6. Vocabulary. Each concept uses the one term in the vocabulary table. Any "Not" word from that table in page copy is a FAIL: quote it.
7. Retired and internal terms. None appear in page copy. Storage names and raw keys never appear in prose. A stored key shows its KEY_LABEL label with the key in a tooltip.
8. Numbers. Money matches usd() or usd0(). Negatives use a true minus sign. Counts use plural(). Units are spaced. Thousands take separators. Durations match dur(). Micros never show.
9. Agreement. The same figure reads the same everywhere it appears: page, navigation, tab counts, drawer badges. Quote every pair that disagrees.
10. Empty values. A missing value renders as a dash in --dim with a reason where one fits, or "not recorded" for an unbacked field. undefined, null, NaN, an empty slot, or an invented zero is a FAIL.
11. Claims. Every control claim is scoped to actions routed through Oxagen or to governed calls. Observe mode reads as recorded, not enforced. No savings, leak, or compliance claim without its evidence.
12. Interface strings. Errors say what happened, then what to do, with no apology. Empty states name the one action. Confirmations state what stays and what goes. Toasts state a past-tense fact.
13. No design reasoning. No string explains the design, weighs its own words, or says what a figure is not.
14. Accessible writing. Links name their destination. Icon buttons carry an aria-label with the action and object. Chip lists carry a hidden separator. No string points by color or position.
15. Spelling. US English throughout.
