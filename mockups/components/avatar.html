    Data display

    Avatar

    An avatar carries a kind (icon, initials, or photo) and a tone, both stored on the record it represents, never chosen at the point of display. People render round. Agents, workspaces, and organizations render as squircles, so a person never reads like the others at a glance. There are five tones: solid, soft, and line follow the theme, and gold and dark gold are the brand gold in two shades. No avatar can carry a color the palette does not already have. One editor sets the avatar of all four records, as the app's ui/avatar-editor.tsx does.

    Root .avx CSS engine.css lines 493, 498 to 510, editor 527 to 554 Used on Agents, Agent card, Approvals, Organization (people, header, workspaces), Account, Spend (evidence dialog), Work orders (Stages), Avatar editor

    PartSelectorWhat it holds
    Shape.avxAn inline grid, centered content, 1px border, fixed inline size set by style.

    Squircle shape.avx.agentA squircle, border-radius:27%, for an agent, a workspace, or an organization.

    Usage

    Use it for
    • Any place a record names a person, an agent, a workspace, or an organization: a table row, a card, a chain, an approval.
    • A place that needs to tell at a glance whether the actor is a person, by shape alone.
    Use something else for
    • A tool or a provider: use its own logo mark, not an avatar shape.
    • A state or an outcome: use a badge, never a colored avatar tone.
    MB

    AvoidA free color outside the five tones, and a radius chosen by hand instead of the shape rule.

    MB

    UseA tone from the house scale and a shape that says "person" before the initials are even read.

    • Shape follows subject, not context: a person is always round, and an agent, a workspace, or an organization is always a squircle, wherever it renders.
    • Tone, icon, font, and photo are stored on the record and read by avatarHtml(). A page never picks a color for an avatar itself.
    • Every place that sets an avatar opens the one editor, openAvatar(). The record changes the shape, the title, the note, and the Remove hint, and nothing else.
    • An avatar beside a name is decorative. The visible text carries the identity, so the avatar itself needs no separate label.

    Content

    • Initials: one to six characters from the record's own text, upper-cased, never invented at render time. With no avatar stored, the tile shows the name's initials (first and last word) on the soft tone.
    • Icon: one glyph from the shared Lucide set, chosen to suggest the agent's job (a rocket for a release manager, a stethoscope for triage), never a literal logo.
    • Alt text: a photo avatar's img carries alt="", since the avatar is decoration next to a visible name.

    Accessibility

    • Every avatar, in every kind, renders with aria-hidden="true", including a photo. A screen reader relies on the name printed beside it, never on the avatar.
    • Tone is never the only signal for anything: it decorates identity, and identity is also carried by the visible initials, icon, or photo, not by color alone.
    • The icon and initials glyphs use currentColor, so they always meet the tone's own contrast pair (--ink on --fg, --fg on --hl, --fg on transparent, --on-gold on --gold at 9.5:1, --on-gold-deep on --gold-deep at 4.66:1).

    Phone

    • An avatar keeps its stored size on a phone. Nothing about .avx has a phone rule of its own.
    • In a card that reflows to a phone width, the avatar stays the same pixel size while the text around it wraps.

    Source

    Where the mockup defines and renders it.

    WhereWhat
    mockups/src/engine.css lines 493, 498 to 510, editor 527 to 554The rules that draw it
    avatarHtml(av, size, shape, extra, letters) at engine.js:339The one avatar renderer: reads {kind, icon|text|url, font, tone} off a record and writes the .avx span. letters is the fallback when the record has no avatar.
    personAv(key, size) at engine.js:353Looks up a person by key and renders them round. 12 calls.
    agentAv(a, size) at engine.js:354Takes an agent object or key and renders it as a squircle. 7 calls.
    initialsOf(name) at engine.js:334The first and last word's initials, upper-cased: the fallback letters and the editor's first draft.
    openAvatar(target) at engine.js:571Opens the one avatar editor for person:<key>, agent:<key>, workspace:<slug>, or org.
    • Rule, in the CSS comment at engine.css 494 to 497: people are round, and agents, workspaces, and organizations are squircles. Five tones: solid, soft, and line from the theme, and gold and dark gold from the brand. No free color, no gradient.
    • .avx.f-serif sets Georgia (with system serif fallbacks), which is the one font family anywhere in the product outside Geist, Space Grotesk, and Monaspace Neon. It is not a departure: it is the operator's own choice of initials font for their avatar, the same way a person picks a photo.
    • .avat (engine.css 493) predates the tone system and has exactly one caller, a fallback avatar in the evidence dialog for an agent id the fixtures do not carry a record for (engine.js 9521). That caller overrides .avat's own circular radius with an inline border-radius:9px, so .avat's own shape never actually appears on screen.

    Findings

    openA dead shape with a live caller

    The components index lists .avat among the rules with no emitter. That is stale: .avat has exactly one caller, the evidence dialog's placeholder avatar for an agent id with no matching record (engine.js 9521). The caller overrides the class's own 52px circular radius with an inline border-radius:9px, so .avat's own definition still renders nothing a person can see. The index's finding should read "one caller, and it overrides the class" rather than "no emitter".

    noteOne legacy shape beside the tone system

    .avat is a flat circle with no kind, no tone, and no shape rule, left over from before avatarHtml(). Its one caller could use agentAv() with a fallback record instead, retiring .avat entirely.

## Procedure

Record PASS, FAIL, or N/A for every check. Cite evidence for each: a file and line in the build, or a DOM selector with its computed style and text.

1. Shape by subject. Every avatar representing a person is a circle, and every avatar representing an agent, a workspace, or an organization is a squircle, with no exception by page or context. Report any avatar whose shape does not match its subject.
2. Tone. Every avatar's fill, glyph color, and border resolve to one of the five tones (solid, soft, line, gold, dark gold), each built from `--fg`, `--ink`, `--hl`, `--border`, `--rule`, `--gold`, `--on-gold`, `--gold-deep`, or `--on-gold-deep`. A raw hex, an rgba, or a gradient on an avatar is a FAIL.
3. One editor. Account, the agent page, the Organization header, and each workspace row open the same avatar editor. A second editor built for one record is a FAIL.
4. One implementation. Every avatar is drawn by one shared component that reads `{kind, tone, shape}` off the record. An avatar assembled inline with its own markup is a FAIL. List every hand-drawn copy, including a flat, toneless fallback circle like `.avat`.
5. Icon set. An icon avatar uses one glyph from the shared icon set, one line weight, in `currentColor`, never a literal brand logo standing in for an agent.
6. Initials. One to six characters from the record's own name or key, upper-cased, sized down as the string lengthens, never invented or truncated inconsistently between call sites.
7. Photo. A photo avatar crops to the shape's radius with `object-fit:cover` and carries `alt=""`, since the name beside it carries the identity.
8. Accessibility. Every avatar is `aria-hidden` or otherwise excluded from the accessibility tree, and the identity it stands for is always also present as visible text beside it.
9. Fonts outside the three faces. Report every font family used inside an avatar. Only Geist, Monaspace Neon, and the operator's own initials-font choice (if the build offers one, as Georgia does here) are expected. Anything else is a FAIL.
10. Tokens. Every color, border, radius, and shadow in the build's version resolves to the tokens listed in the Tokens section of the reference page. A raw hex, rgb, or named color is a FAIL.
11. Type. Text computes to Geist and ids, keys, and code compute to Monaspace Neon, at the sizes and weights the reference page's examples render. Space Grotesk anywhere in the component is a FAIL.
12. Themes. Repeat every visual check in the light theme. A part that loses its border, its contrast, or its meaning in one theme is a FAIL.
13. Phone. At 390 by 844 with a touch pointer: An avatar keeps its stored size on a phone. Nothing about `.avx` has a phone rule of its own. In a card that reflows to a phone width, the avatar stays the same pixel size while the text around it wraps.
14. Accessibility. Every avatar, in every kind, renders with `aria-hidden="true"`, including a photo. A screen reader relies on the name printed beside it, never on the avatar. Tone is never the only signal for anything: it decorates identity, and identity is also carried by the visible initials, icon, or photo, not by color alone. The icon and initials glyphs use `currentColor`, so they always meet the tone's own contrast pair (`--ink` on `--fg`, `--fg` on `--hl`, `--fg` on transparent, `--on-gold` on `--gold` at 9.5:1, `--on-gold-deep` on `--gold-deep` at 4.66:1).
15. One implementation. The build draws the avatar from one shared component. List every place that draws it by hand, with its file and line.
