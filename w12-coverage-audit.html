Method

How each link was checked

  1. Read Appendix F and §14 first, so the audit measured against the ten pages the specification keeps — seven at workspace scope, three at organization scope — and against the fact that Approvals is a panel, Account is a dialog, and the seven sign-in flows are not pages.
  2. Opened every artifact the coverage table links to and read the whole file, not the first screen. Each mockup declares its screens and its per-screen states in data, so the claim in the table can be compared against the code that renders it.
  3. Probed for the states by name in each file — loaded, empty, loading, error, denied — plus the phone reflow and the three-state theme pattern (:root, the guarded prefers-color-scheme block, and [data-theme]).
  4. Probed for the sub-areas the coverage table names explicitly, not just the page title. A row that says “Tools (registry, connections, mandates, policy, kill switches, assurance)” is only covered when all six are present somewhere behind its links.
  5. Built the addendum in the same brand and conventions, and appended its link to the rows it covers — never replacing a sibling’s link.
  6. Drove the addendum in a headless browser: every route in every state, every tab, every dialog and every frame rendered; the document’s horizontal overflow measured at 400 px on each one; and all three theme paths resolved to a real background and foreground. This is the step that catches what a syntax check cannot.

A keyword probe alone would be weak evidence. It is used here only to confirm presence after the file had been read, and the depth judgements below come from the reading, not the counts.

What changed while the audit ran

Two gaps that closed under the audit’s hands

This audit began against a version of the table in which four rows were thin and two scenarios were unmocked. It is worth recording, because it is the only interesting thing that happened.

At the first pass: four thin rows

Spend and Billing had no dedicated mockup; Organization and Audit were reached by a link but not built out. W8 and W10 — the two scenarios that own exactly those four pages — were still not started. The addendum was built to close them.

At the last pass: none

W8 and W10 published while the addendum was being written. Both are substantial and both carry every sub-area their rows name. The four rows are now covered twice over, and the addendum is a second cover rather than the only one.

One finding the audit had to withdraw. A keyword probe reported “spend by operator” as absent from every artifact. It is present in W8, as the Operators view under an Attribution tab, backed by a DB.operators rollup and a per-operator table. The probe was looking for the wrong words. The reading corrected it, which is the reason the method puts reading first and probing second.

The addendum

Why a twelfth artifact still earns its place

With no gaps left to fill, The Ten Pages is not a patch. It is the one artifact where all ten pages Appendix F keeps sit behind a single shell and a single state control, which is what makes the claim in this audit’s title checkable in one place rather than across eleven files.

What it carries

Seven workspace pages and three organization pages with their tabs — thirty-eight in all — the Approvals panel on both Fleet and Run, the frame player, the mandate detail, the assistant in all three states, the Account dialog, notifications, the command menu, and twenty-six dialogs, each one an action the specification names.

What it is for

Switching one control walks every page through loaded, empty, loading, error and denied without leaving the app shell, and the phone toggle reflows all of it to 400 px. It is the regression surface for the next change to the specification: if a page loses a state, it shows here first.

Honesty

What this audit does not claim

Coverage is not craft

A row is marked covered when the screen and the state exist and read correctly against the specification. That is not a judgement that the screen is the best version of itself. Several rows now carry four links; a later pass could choose one per row and retire the others.

Figures differ between artifacts

The twelve were built in parallel and made independent choices inside the shared demo customer: agent counts per workspace, the current policy version, and the month’s totals are not identical from one to the next. The organization, the two workspaces, the repositories and production branches, the three operators and the five named agents agree everywhere.

Eleven were read, one was driven

The eleven scenario mockups were audited as source: routes, state branches and theme blocks compared against Appendix F. They were not opened in a browser. A rendering fault their source does not reveal would not have been caught here — and the addendum proves such faults exist.

What driving the addendum caught

Two defects that read correctly as source. Horizontal overflow at 400 px on Fleet (772 px) and Billing (179 px): a grid track written 1fr instead of minmax(0,1fr) let a wide table push the body sideways, because grid children default to min-width:auto. And gold encoding state: the run filter marked its selected option gold, and six repeated actions on Spend were all gold against a rule of one primary per screen. Both fixed and re-measured; the same two traps are worth checking in the other eleven.

The section’s own test is the one applied

§19 says every row must carry at least one link before the section is complete. Fifteen of fifteen do, each one verified, so the status line is set to complete. No other reading of “complete” was assumed.
